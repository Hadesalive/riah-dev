# Surface brief: riah.dev (all marketing pages; home is primary)

Scope: whole marketing site, Persuade mode. Audience: ministries, NGOs/health programmes, banks/fintechs/businesses, hotels (weighted equally). Job: understand what RIAH does in seconds, trust it, request a consultation. Proof: none supplied; no invented clients, numbers or photos.

User feedback driving the redesign (2026-10-07): unclear what RIAH does, everything looks equal, too plain/no imagery, network diagram confusing.

## Direction contract

THESIS: riah.dev is a painted agent kiosk: Freetown's hand-painted mobile-money and telecom signage, applied to an engineering firm. The site reads like a shopfront you understand from across the street. It refuses the pale hairline list page (previous build) and the SaaS dark hero with icon-card grid.

OWN-WORLD: Flat paint fields that own whole bands: kiosk blue #2563EB dominant, sign yellow #FACC15 lettering and primary action, slate #0F172A ink and dark boards, emerald #10B981 money/OK boards, whitewash #F8FAFC wall. Boards have an inner painted pinstripe border and faint brush texture. Display lettering is showcard (Bowlby One) with a painted drop shade, set in mixed case as a legibility adaptation for non-specialist readers (step names keep caps); showcard only at sign scale, small titles in body bold; body is Atkinson Hyperlegible Next. Authored flat-painted pictograms with slate outlines, one per service.

STORY: The visitor reads "Networks. Servers. SMS. Payments." on the big sign, scans the menu board of services in plain words, finds their sector's board, sees how a project runs, and presses the yellow button to request a consultation.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Painted fascia header (blue band, white logo, yellow "Request a consultation"). Below, a full-width blue signboard: left seven columns carry the four-word sign lettering at display scale in yellow with slate drop shade, a one-sentence plain description in white, a yellow primary button and white secondary. Right five columns hold a slate menu board titled "What we do" listing seven services with painted pictograms, each a link. Lettering is brush-revealed once on load.
